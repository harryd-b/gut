# Referee Report: JOIN-4b (contact-geometric repair of KNOT-Q)

**Summary verdict.** The derivation's destructive core is sound: F1, F2, F4, and F5 check out against the literature, F3 checks out with a sharpened attribution (Colin 2001, universal tightness included), and the demolition of KNOT-Q's Bennequin mechanism, trefoil clause, and Arf clause is correct and, if anything, understated. The two overreaches are BOX 2's word "forced" — the Giroux–Honda classification of tight structures on circle bundles contains tight structures on T¹Σ_g *not* obtained from ξ_can by torsion insertion, so "only tightness-compatible quantized deformation" is UNPROVEN as a universal claim — and BOX 4's "single missing object," which is well-posed only as internal bookkeeping relative to the postulated GAP-4 bridge; no framework in the literature defines a map from bulk contact structures to boundary nets, and the kill as *registered* quantifies over knot classes, which the repaired bridge no longer decorates. Net: the negative results stand; the positive re-scoping is conditionally correct; the conjecture's testability reduction is fair only after re-registration of the kill.

## 1. Adjudication A (F1, F2, BOX 1)

**F1: CORRECT.** A transverse knot in any contact 3-manifold has a standard tubular neighborhood (contact neighborhood theorem), and the Lutz twist is a local modification in that neighborhood. No hypothesis on self-linking number, genus, or homology class is required, and no theorem of the form "Lutz twist requires sl ≥ bound" exists. The Bennequin inequality sl(K) ≤ 2g(K)−1 constrains *invariants of transverse knots in tight structures*; it says nothing about admissibility of surgery-type modifications. KNOT-Q's "Bennequin budget" mechanism is indeed a category error — the deriver is right, and Geiges §4.3 is the right citation.

**F2: CORRECT.** Both the half (π) and full (2π) Lutz twist along a transverse knot produce an overtwisted structure: the tube contains a radius where the twisting angle passes through π, and the meridional disk there is overtwisted. The full twist preserves the homotopy class of the plane field yet is still overtwisted — this is the standard counterexample stock for h-principle failure. On the mandated caveats: (i) there is **no "tight Lutz twist" in dimension 3** — the tight analogue of a Lutz twist is precisely torsion insertion along a *torus* (F3), not along any knot; (ii) "Lutz twist along a fiber" is a red herring for this carrier: the S¹-fibers of T¹Σ_g are *Legendrian* for ξ_can (the canonical 1-form vanishes on fiber directions), not transverse, so no knot-Lutz along fibers is even defined without first transversalizing, after which F2 applies.

**BOX 1: CORRECT.** The dichotomy is airtight for knot-attached twists. Minor repair: the half twist changes the homotopy class of the plane field, the full twist does not — irrelevant to the dichotomy but should be stated. "Kill HANGS on the literal reading" is the right adjudication: quantization is undefined, not zero.

## 2. Adjudication B (F3–F5, BOX 2)

**F3: CORRECT WITH REPAIR.** Colin, *Une infinité de structures de contact tendues sur les variétés toroïdales* (2001), proves every closed orientable irreducible **toroidal** 3-manifold carries infinitely many **universally tight** contact structures, via gluing/insertion along incompressible tori, distinguished by Giroux's torsion. So universal tightness *is* preserved and the attribution is Colin (using Giroux's torsion invariant), not Colin–Giroux–Honda (their joint work is the later coarse-classification/finiteness program). T¹Σ_g (g ≥ 2, as required for hyperbolic base — the derivation should say so explicitly; g = 1 gives T³, Giroux/Kanda territory) is toroidal, so GAP-1 resolves in the deriver's favor. Technical repair: the vertical torus over a geodesic is not automatically pre-Lagrangian for ξ_can; it must first be isotoped to pre-Lagrangian/convex position, which is standard but should be stated.

**F4: CORRECT.** In a Seifert fibered space every two-sided incompressible surface is isotopic to a vertical or horizontal one (Waldhausen/Hatcher). Horizontal surfaces in a closed Seifert space exist only if the Euler number vanishes; e(T¹Σ_g) = ±(2g−2) ≠ 0. Independently, a horizontal surface is a branched cover of the base, so over a hyperbolic base it has χ < 0 and cannot be a torus. Two independent exclusions; vertical incompressible tori are exactly π⁻¹(c) for c essential and simple (embeddedness of the torus forces simplicity — this is what powers §5). GAP-2 closes.

**F5: CORRECT.** The literature confirms: even *half* Giroux torsion along a non-essential (compressible) torus in a closed manifold implies overtwistedness (stated e.g. in the recent bordered-contact-invariant literature; the mechanism is folklore-standard — compressing disk plus torsion layer yields an overtwisted disk). GAP-3 closes.

**BOX 2: CORRECT WITH REPAIR — the word "forced" overreaches.** Within the operation class {knot-Lutz, torus torsion insertion}, the locus is forced: knot-Lutz always overtwists (F2), compressible-torus torsion overtwists (F5), so only incompressible = vertical tori survive (F4), quantized by n. But tight structures on circle bundles over closed surfaces are *classified* (Honda, *Classification of tight contact structures II*, JDG 2000; Giroux independently), and the classification contains finitely many additional tight structures (e.g. S¹-invariant ones with different dividing-set/Euler-class data) **not** obtained from ξ_can by torsion insertion. So "admissible quantized twist data on tight T¹Σ_g = torsion on vertical tori" is true only under the re-scoping "quantized deformations of ξ_can within the considered operation class." That the derivation never proves "every tightness-compatible localized deformation is a torsion insertion" must be flagged: the forcing is proved for the menu considered, not for all conceivable decorations. UNPROVEN as a universal; CORRECT as re-scoped.

## 3. Adjudication C (BOX 3)

**Mechanism (i): CORRECT, with a well-posedness caveat the deriver should own.** Seifert genus presupposes a null-homologous knot; in T¹Σ_g with H₁ = ℤ^{2g} ⊕ ℤ/(2g−2), most knots are not null-homologous, so clause (a) as registered is silently restricted to its domain of definition. On that domain the chain is valid: genus-0 Seifert surface = embedded disk ⟹ K null-homotopic ⟹ π∘K contractible in Σ ⟹ (by the refereed locality theorem) no hyperbolic anchor ⟹ Q_visible = 0. Note the honest gap the deriver correctly preserved: this kills *coupling-visible* charge only; if KNOT-Q's Q means total charge, only invisibility is shown. **Mechanism (ii) is conditional on BOX 2's re-scope** — "any twist near a contractible class needs a compressible torus" holds only within the proven operation class. So (i) carries the weight; the genus-0 kill indeed cannot fire. Overall: **CORRECT WITH REPAIR** (mechanism replaced, Bennequin refuted — both adjudicated correctly).

## 4. Adjudication D (Ghys; H₁; clauses b, c)

**Ghys attribution: CORRECT.** Ghys (*Knots and dynamics*, ICM 2006): the unit tangent bundle of the modular orbifold PSL(2,ℤ)\PSL(2,ℝ) is homeomorphic to the trefoil complement in S³, and periodic geodesics there are modular/Lorenz knots. Wrong carrier for KNOT-Q, exactly as the deriver says: noncompact quotient orbifold vs compact T¹Σ_g, and "trefoil" has no canonical meaning in T¹Σ_g. Clause (b)'s trefoil-minimality: **WRONG as registered**; deriver's numerology diagnosis CORRECT.

**H₁ computation: CORRECT.** π₁(T¹Σ_g) is the central extension with relation ∏[aᵢ,bᵢ] = z^{±(2g−2)}; abelianizing gives H₁ ≅ ℤ^{2g} ⊕ ℤ/(2g−2), fiber class of order |e| = |2−2g| = 2g−2. Sign is convention; order is right. Clause (c): Arf requires a null-homologous knot (Seifert form); charged Reeb orbits (geodesic lifts) are generically non-null-homologous (even lifts of separating geodesics land in fiber torsion). Deriver's ill-typedness verdict: **CORRECT**; clause (c) **WRONG as registered**, torsion parity (−1)^{en} is a twist invariant, not a knot invariant.

## 5. Adjudication E (BOX 4, §5)

**§5 (simple geodesics only): CORRECT and genuinely discriminating** — the boundary locality theorem anchors on fix(γ) for *any* hyperbolic γ, while embedded vertical tori exist only over simple classes; the contact side predicts strictly less, which is a real (unregistered) test.

**BOX 4: UNPROVEN / conditionally fair, and the category mismatch must be flagged.** Two problems. (i) "The boundary-net action of a Giroux-torsion insertion" is not a defined object in any existing framework: there is no functor from bulk contact 3-manifolds to boundary conformal nets in the literature; the holonomy principle (GAP-4) is a postulate identifying a contact winding number with a U(1) defect winding, and the "missing object" is well-posed only *relative to that postulate*. The deriver flags GAP-4 honestly but then writes as if "compute e" were a determinate open problem; it is a modeling task, not a computation, until the bridge is defined. (ii) Kill semantics: as registered, the kill quantifies over *knot classes*; under the repair, decorations are tori/curve classes, so "zero charge for every knot class" cannot literally evaluate — the deriver's own BOX 1 ("kill HANGS") is in tension with BOX 4's "kill fires iff e = 0," which presupposes an unregistered re-reading. The verdict INCONCLUSIVE is right, but the reduction "testability ⟺ e ≠ 0" is fair only after (a) re-registering the kill over essential-simple-curve classes and (b) admitting BOX 2's operation-class restriction. The symbolic check α_n ∧ dα_n = 2πn·dV is correct (cos²+sin² collapses the wedge) but measures torsion, not any boundary quantity.
