## 1. (A) Theorem 1 and the orbit count — CORRECT WITH REPAIR

**Rigidity mechanics.** Monoidality per geodesic plus F(γ,0) = 0 and the involution give F(γ,n) = n·F(γ,1) =: e_γ·n in the sector lattice ℤ. Fine. GAP-2's automorphism claim is right as stated: fusion-preserving automorphisms of the compactified-boson sector lattice that preserve h_q = q²/2 and the braiding are group automorphisms of ℤ, hence ±id (q ↦ −q preserves e^{iπq₁q₂î} and h_q; nothing else is available).

**The loophole.** "±id" is not innocent. For every nonseparating γ there exists φ ∈ MCG with φ(γ) = γ preserving orientation. If the boundary implementation of such a φ were −id, equivariance forces e_γ = −e_γ, i.e. **e = 0**, destroying everything downstream (and contradicting P′). Theorem 1 implicitly assumes the implementation is +id exactly on the oriented-curve stabilizer and −id exactly on orientation-reversing classes (where it is then absorbed by conjugation-equivariance). This is natural — the charge is attached to the *oriented* curve — but it is an axiom, not a consequence, and must be stated. Repair: add "the implementing automorphism is id whenever φ preserves γ with orientation" to (iii).

**Orbit count.** Correct. Free homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves biject with simple closed geodesics (unique geodesic representative; simple class → simple geodesic), and the change-of-coordinates principle (Farb–Margalit §1.3) gives transitivity on nonseparating curves and classification of separating curves by the splitting genus h = 1,…,⌊g/2⌋. Given B2's negative result (no canonical crossing phase) there is indeed no axiom linking orbits — and separating curves have î = 0 with everything, so crossings could not see them anyway. Honest count: **1 + ⌊g/2⌋ independent constants**. Correct, and correctly deflating.

## 2. (B) Braiding closure — CORRECT

Verified: the fiber tangent ∂_θ lies in ker α everywhere (α has no dθ term) — fibers of T¹Σ are Legendrian, a standard fact reproduced correctly by the model. Wall T_γ = {t = 0} is ξ-tangent exactly where cos θ = 0, i.e. the two unit-normal circles; two walls through a crossing mark two antipodal pairs {φᵢ ± π/2} on the fiber, and two distinct antipodal pairs always cyclically alternate — the "four alternating marked points" claim is correct. The crossing angle is a continuous, unquantized datum (varies with metric/curve choice), so no discrete local invariant exists from which to manufacture a canonical U(1) phase; and imposing the boundary braiding phase as an axiom would presuppose the bulk–boundary identification being derived. Circularity verdict: **correct**. The salvage P′ ⟺ (e−1)n even ∀n ⟺ e odd: correct. One deflationary remark the derivation should own: **P′ is exactly equivalent to B5's mod-1 spin matching** (both ⟺ e odd, since e odd ⟹ 8 | e²−1). They are one constraint presented twice, not independent corroboration.

## 4. (D) THE CRUX — S(n): CORRECT WITH REPAIR on the arithmetic; H-QUAD **UNPROVEN** as normalized input to W

**(i) GAP-4 closes.** The winding angle of ξ ∩ TT_t across the layer is literally 2πnt, linear in t; winding across [0, k/n] = k. Correct with no further argument needed.

**(ii) The tower is misstated — but the error is numerically harmless, and fixing it *strengthens* the case.** "Reeb σ-directed" means sin(2πnt) = 0: t = k/(2n), k = 0,…,2n — **2n+1 tori**, with Reeb = (−1)^k∂_σ, exactly as the brief suspects. The derivation's t = k/n tower is the sub-tower where Reeb = +∂_σ. Restricting to it is canonical *given the orientation of γ* (decorations are oriented; "Reeb positively parallel to γ̇" is orientation-natural), and conjugation-equivariance is respected (γ ↦ γ̄ swaps the sub-towers, S unchanged, matching q ↦ −q with h even in q). Moreover — the derivation misses its own best argument — the −∂_σ sub-tower consists of **interior** tori at t = (2k+1)/(2n) with half-integer framings (2k+1)/2, whose plain unweighted sum is Σ(2k+1)/2 = **n²/2 with no endpoint convention at all** (verified). Both orientation classes independently give n²/2. But the **full** tower (framings k/2, trapezoid weights) gives **n²** (verified) — the sum of the two classes. So "n²/2" is robust *per orientation class*, and the class choice is orientation-canonical. Repair required: state the true tower, the restriction, and its justification.

**(iii) The ½-convention.** The brief's proposed selector — t ↦ 1−t invariance — **fails**: under framings k ↦ n−k, the no-endpoint sum n(n−1)/2 and the both-full-endpoint sum n(n+1)/2 are *each already invariant* (symmetric ranges; verified). Only the two one-sided conventions (count t=0 only vs t=1 only) swap. So reflection symmetry narrows four-plus conventions to three, not one; it does **not** derive n²/2. The convention that *is* uniquely selected — and I verified this — is **gluing/concatenation additivity**: stacking torsion-n and torsion-m layers at a shared interface, the half-endpoint sum satisfies S(n) + S(m|shifted) = (n+m)²/2 exactly; full endpoints over-count by n (the interface counted twice), no-endpoints under-count by n. The derivation's "boundary tori are shared interfaces" gloss is precisely this trapezoid identity, so the ½-convention **does have principled content** once stated as: S is the unique convention consistent under layer concatenation. That is a real repair, not a rescue I invented — but the derivation must prove it, not gesture.

**Bottom line on H-QUAD.** After repairs, "the oriented Reeb-tower trapezoid sum equals n²/2" is genuine geometry, not pure convention: the tower is canonical (given orientation), the framings are canonical, and the weighting is forced by concatenation consistency. What is **not** canonical is the claim that *this* quantity, at *this* normalization, is "the" bulk quadratic invariant. The full tower with the identical concatenation logic gives n². Any positive multiple c·S is equally canonical. H-QUAD as used by W ("S(n) is canonical *and correctly normalized to boundary-weight units*") is **UNPROVEN**, and the normalization is exactly where the desired answer enters.
