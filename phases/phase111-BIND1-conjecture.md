## 1. Statement

**BIND-1.** When two carriers c₁, c₂ (charges Q₁, Q₂) intersect, the bound composite is carried not by the disjoint union of c₁ and c₂ but by the closed geodesic c₁₂ in the free homotopy class of the concatenation c₁ · c₂ joined at a crossing point. The binding energy of the composite is the length defect

Δℓ = ℓ(c₁₂) − ℓ(c₁) − ℓ(c₂),

read through the mass conjecture MASS-1: M_composite = M(ℓ(c₁₂)) ≠ M(ℓ₁) + M(ℓ₂). No dynamics is claimed; the defect is a kinematic function of the fixed geometry (data, not dynamics).

**Supporting geometric fact (to be nailed by derivation):** on a closed hyperbolic surface, the geodesic representative of a product class of two crossing geodesics is strictly SHORTER than ℓ₁ + ℓ₂ (the concatenated curve has corners; tightening removes them), with defect depending on the crossing angle via trace identities. Sign note: this 2D mechanism yields a mass DEFECT (attractive, nuclear-style binding). The operator's linked-knots picture — composites forced LONGER (QCD-style mass excess, proton ≫ Σ quarks) — plausibly requires genuine 3D linking on the original 3-manifold, out of reach of the boundary-circle dictionary; recorded as the two-signs scope note, not resolved here.

## 3. First test, worked through: where the fusion product lives

The refereed record's fusion entry (E4) is same-axis only: both charges on ONE geodesic, ΔD = Q₁Q₂ℓ/π, totals-only in A(I). Cross-axis fusion has NO refereed rule — the record is currently silent on which carrier the product of two crossing defects rides. BIND-1 is a conjecture about exactly that silence. If a cross-axis fusion rule is ever derived in the arena (A(I), the joint arena of E6, or the discrete formulation) and it selects the concatenated class, BIND-1's mechanism is real; if it selects the disjoint union (totals-only, no carrier merge), BIND-1 dies.
