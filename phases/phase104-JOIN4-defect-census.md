## 3. The scope audit (Step 1) — partially executed in-draft; REFEREE PASS REQUIRED

The kill rests on two pillars with different scopes:

**Pillar A — base-inertness — is state-scoped.** Its hypotheses are explicit: γ-invariance of the state (ω∘Ad U(γ) = ω), faithfulness on the leg (with the spectral-gap condition), canonical/leg-aligned data. A defect state violates the first hypothesis by construction. Pillar A says nothing about non-invariant states.

**Pillar B — [R_Γ] rigidity — is algebra-scoped, and it reaches further than the session first hoped.** The pillar closes all *inner* implementations: base motion from unitaries of 𝒞 lands in the full group [R_Γ], which contains no continuous one-parameter group. Consequence [derived in-draft; one step from recorded statements; **needs referee confirmation**]:

> **Normal-state closure.** Every faithful normal state on 𝒞 — including any would-be defect state in the vacuum folium — has modular data already covered by the phase-103 census (vacuum flow ∘ inner cocycle, closed by Pillar B). **The defect escape cannot be realized by any normal state on 𝒞.**

This is not a defeat of §2(c); it is its sharpening, and it lands exactly on the referee's wording. To break γ-invariance in a way the census does not reach, the defect must change the **folium**: an inequivalent GNS representation — a **superselection sector** — whose standard pairs are precisely "non-leg-aligned pairs whose modular unitaries lie outside 𝒞." The framework's matter-as-defect ontology and the algebraic notion of a charged/solitonic sector (DHR; solitonic representations of conformal nets, Kawahigashi–Longo–Müger-adjacent technology) are the same shape. The referee's "no computational technology" is now qualified: soliton-sector technology *exists* for finite-index and orbifold conformal nets; what does not exist is its Fuchsian-equivariant version.
